<p>Flood risk data in the GTA is often inaccessible or outdated, leaving most residents, urban planners, municipalities and government agencies without the information needed to take preventive measures.<br><br>Addressing this critical gap, research by Nicole Gordon, [](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ACoAADcvqX4BXe-F39Km8BWJ2cUNSJ-LfiLroQM)[Emily Majeed](https://www.linkedin.com/in/emily-majeed-7ba0a421a/), [](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ACoAAAxhvTsB2JmXTp2TqO8WQi0PsBA1tTUsQvE)[Nidhi Subramanyam](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nidhi-subramanyam-36369b59/) and [](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ACoAAAEQsBIB743mZgtI-zlMv4nVB-uwW6b5uYk)[Robert Soden](https://www.linkedin.com/in/robertsoden/) with the [Toronto Climate Observatory](https://www.linkedin.com/company/toronto-climate-observatory/) and the [School of Cities, University of Toronto](https://www.linkedin.com/company/uoftcities/)' Urban Challenge Grant has mapped out available flood data across the GTA.<br><br>This highlights the urgent need for regularly updated, community-informed information and reveals that most publicly available data is outdated, lacks transparency, and often overlooks the impacts on vulnerable communities. As climate change accelerates, we must ensure our flood data ecosystem is robust and equitable.<br><br>Explore the map, search for data in your area, and contribute to building a flood-resilient GTA: [https://lnkd.in/g9eFSxPp](https://lnkd.in/g9eFSxPp)</p>
